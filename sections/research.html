Strategic Misdirection: Attempts to Protect Privacy with Made-Up Email Addresses

S. Ahmed, E. Rader, S. Patil

Investigated why people use made-up email addresses when signing up for services. The study examines how people balance unwanted contact, privacy concerns, and the risk of losing account access.

Under review · ACM CHI

Perceptions and Practices of Unintended Recipients of Misdirected Email Messages

S. Ahmed, E. Rader, S. Patil

Studied how people respond to emails sent to them by mistake, including whether they delete, ignore, reply to, or report the message. The survey examines how recipients interpret their responsibilities when a message contains someone else’s information.

I designed the survey, recruited participants across two platforms, and built the analysis pipeline. The assistant prototype is in development.

Under review · ACM CSCW

Replication: The Westin Categories, Behavioral Intentions, and Consequences

S. Ahmed, E. Rader, S. Patil

Replicated a 2014 study to examine whether the Westin privacy categories predict people’s stated willingness to disclose information. I extended the analysis to compare decisions about one’s own data with decisions involving other people’s data.

In our analysis, the privacy categories had limited predictive value. Expected negative consequences were associated with disclosure intentions, including in scenarios involving other people’s information.

As first author, I designed the study, collected and analyzed the data, and wrote the paper.
